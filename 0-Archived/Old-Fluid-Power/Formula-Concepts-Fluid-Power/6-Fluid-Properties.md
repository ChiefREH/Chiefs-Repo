# 6. Fluid Properties

Fluid properties describe the physical characteristics of liquids and gases used in fluid power systems. Understanding these properties is important when selecting hydraulic fluids, evaluating system performance, and determining how fluids behave under different operating conditions.

Two important fluid properties are density and specific gravity. Density describes how much mass is contained within a given volume, while specific gravity compares the density of a fluid to the density of water.

## 6.1 Density

Density is the amount of mass contained within a given volume of a substance.

In hydraulic systems, density is important because it affects the weight and inertia of the fluid. Different hydraulic fluids can have different densities depending on their composition and temperature.

**Formula:**

$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$

Where:

- $\rho$ = Density
- $m$ = Mass
- $V$ = Volume

Common density units include:

- Kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³)
- Grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³)
- Pounds mass per cubic foot (lbm/ft³)

Density is calculated by dividing the mass of a substance by its volume. A fluid with more mass in the same volume has a higher density.

### Example: Calculating Fluid Density

A sample of hydraulic fluid has a mass of 8 kg and occupies a volume of 10 liters. Calculate its density.

Convert liters to cubic meters:

$10 \text{ L} = 0.01 \text{ m}^3$

Calculate density:

$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$

$\rho = \frac{8}{0.01}$

**Answer: 800 kg/m³**

The hydraulic fluid has a density of 800 kilograms per cubic meter.

## 6.2 Specific Gravity

Specific gravity (SG) is the ratio of a fluid's density to the density of water at a specified reference temperature.

Specific gravity indicates whether a fluid is more or less dense than water for the same volume.

**Formula:**

$SG = \frac{\text{Fluid Density}}{\text{Water Density}}$

Where:

- $SG$ = Specific gravity
- Fluid Density = Density of the fluid being measured
- Water Density = Reference density of water

Specific gravity is a dimensionless value, meaning it has no units. The density units cancel when one density is divided by another.

For most introductory fluid power calculations, water density can be approximated as:

- 1,000 kg/m³
- 1 g/cm³
- 62.4 lbm/ft³

### Understanding Specific Gravity

- **SG = 1.0:** The fluid has approximately the same density as water.
- **SG greater than 1.0:** The fluid is denser than water.
- **SG less than 1.0:** The fluid is less dense than water.

For example, a hydraulic oil with a specific gravity of 0.85 is approximately 85% as dense as water.

### Example: Calculating Specific Gravity

A hydraulic oil has a density of 850 kg/m³. The density of water is 1,000 kg/m³. Calculate the specific gravity of the hydraulic oil.

$SG = \frac{\text{Fluid Density}}{\text{Water Density}}$

Substitute the known values:

$SG = \frac{850}{1,000}$

**Answer: SG = 0.85**

The hydraulic oil has a specific gravity of 0.85. This means it is approximately 85% as dense as water.

## 6.3 Comparing Density and Specific Gravity

Density and specific gravity are related, but they are not the same measurement.

| Property | Density | Specific Gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Mass per unit volume | Ratio of fluid density to water density |
| Units | kg/m³, g/cm³, lbm/ft³ | No units |
| Purpose | Measures how much mass occupies a volume | Compares fluid density to water |
| Reference | No reference substance required | Water |

## 6.4 Important Concepts

- Density describes the amount of mass contained in a given volume.
- Density is calculated by dividing mass by volume.
- A fluid with greater mass in the same volume has a higher density.
- Density is commonly expressed in kg/m³, g/cm³, or lbm/ft³.
- Specific gravity compares a fluid's density to the density of water.
- Specific gravity is dimensionless and has no units.
- A specific gravity greater than 1.0 indicates a fluid is denser than water.
- A specific gravity less than 1.0 indicates a fluid is less dense than water.
- Water density is commonly approximated as 1,000 kg/m³ for introductory calculations.
- Density changes with temperature; liquids generally become less dense as temperature increases.
- Specific gravity is useful when comparing hydraulic oils and other fluids.
- Density and specific gravity help identify and compare the physical properties of fluids.
