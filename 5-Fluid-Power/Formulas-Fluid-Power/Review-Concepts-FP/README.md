## Hydraulic Pumps Create Flow

A hydraulic pump's primary function is to move fluid.

> **A pump creates flow, not pressure.**

Pressure develops only when the flowing fluid encounters resistance.

### Example

If a pump is moving oil through an open return line back to a reservoir:

- Flow is present
- Pressure is very low

If the flow encounters a load:

- Pressure increases
- Force becomes available

### Key Concept

**Pump → Flow → Resistance → Pressure**

---

## Pressure Develops When Flow Meets Resistance

Pressure is a measure of resistance to fluid flow.

Without resistance:

- Flow exists
- Pressure remains low

With resistance:

- Pressure increases
- Force becomes available

### Example

When a hydraulic cylinder contacts a heavy load:

- Pump flow continues
- System pressure rises
- Cylinder force increases

### Key Concept

> **Flow creates movement. Resistance creates pressure.**

## Hydraulic Oil Is Considered Incompressible

Liquids can technically compress slightly under very high pressure, but for most industrial calculations hydraulic oil is considered incompressible.

### Why It Matters

Because hydraulic fluid does not compress significantly:

- Motion is predictable
- Force transmission is efficient
- Position control is accurate

### Key Concept

> **Hydraulic systems are precise because liquids are essentially incompressible.**

---

## Air Is Compressible

Unlike hydraulic oil, air can be compressed.

This property allows energy to be stored in compressed air systems.

### Effects of Compressibility

#### Advantages

- Energy storage
- Shock absorption
- Simple system design

#### Disadvantages

- Less precise motion
- Reduced rigidity
- Positioning errors

### Key Concept

> **Pneumatic systems sacrifice precision for simplicity and speed.**

---

## Pneumatic Systems Use Compressed Air

Pneumatic systems use compressed air as their working fluid.

### Common Components

- Air compressors
- Air receivers
- Regulators
- Valves
- Air cylinders

### Typical Operating Pressure

**80–120 psi**

### Key Concept

> **Hydraulics use liquid. Pneumatics use compressed air.**

---

## Atmospheric Pressure

The atmosphere exerts pressure on all objects at Earth's surface.

At sea level:

`Atmospheric Pressure ≈ 14.7 psi`

This value is important when converting between gauge pressure and absolute pressure.

### Example

```text
100 psig + 14.7 = 114.7 psia
```

### Key Concept

> **14.7 psi represents atmospheric pressure at sea level.**

---

## Gauge Pressure

Most pressure gauges measure pressure relative to atmospheric pressure.

This measurement is called:

**Gauge Pressure (psig)**

### Example

A gauge reading of:

```text
100 psig
```

means the system is operating **100 psi above atmospheric pressure**.

### Key Concept

> **Gauge pressure does not include atmospheric pressure.**

---

## Absolute Pressure

Absolute pressure references a perfect vacuum (zero pressure).

### Formula

`Pabs = Pgauge + 14.7`

### Example

```text
50 psig + 14.7 = 64.7 psia
```

### Key Concept

> **Gas-law calculations require absolute pressure.**

---

## Absolute Temperature

Gas laws also require absolute temperature.

### Common Scales

- Rankine (°R)
- Kelvin (K)

### Not Acceptable for Gas-Law Calculations

- Fahrenheit (°F)
- Celsius (°C)

These must first be converted to an absolute temperature scale.

### Key Concept

> **Gas-law calculations require both absolute pressure and absolute temperature.**

---

## Hydraulic vs. Pneumatic Systems

| Hydraulic Systems | Pneumatic Systems |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Use liquid | Use compressed air |
| Higher force capability | Lower force capability |
| Fluid considered incompressible | Air is compressible |
| More precise control | Less precise control |
| Typical pressure: 500-5000+ psi | Typical pressure: 80-120 psi |

### Key Concept

> **Hydraulics excel at force. Pneumatics excel at simplicity and speed.**

# Essential Fluid Power Relationships

Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

---

`F = P × A`

---

## Flow Creates Speed

`V = Q ÷ A`

---

## Pressure + Flow Create Power

---

## Pumps Create Flow
