# Pascal's Law

## Principle
Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

## Detailed Explanation
Pascal's Law is the foundation of hydraulic power systems. When a hydraulic pump creates pressure, that pressure is available throughout the entire fluid column. Pressure does not flow; fluid flows. Pressure is resistance to flow.

Because pressure is transmitted equally, hydraulic systems can multiply force by increasing the output area.

## Example
Input piston area = 2 in²
Pressure = 500 psi

Output force:

`F = P × A`

`F = 500 × 2 = 1,000 lb`

## Applications
- Hydraulic presses
- Vehicle lifts
- Hydraulic jacks
- Injection molding machines

## Common Mistakes
- Confusing pressure with flow
- Assuming higher pressure means higher speed
- Ignoring system losses

## Practice Problems
1. A hydraulic system operates at 750 psi. What pressure exists elsewhere in the circuit?
2. If a confined fluid is pressurized to 2,000 psi, what pressure exists at a cylinder 10 feet away?
3. Explain why hydraulic lifts can raise heavy loads with relatively small input forces.

## Answers
1. 750 psi
2. 2,000 psi
3. Pressure is transmitted equally throughout the fluid.
